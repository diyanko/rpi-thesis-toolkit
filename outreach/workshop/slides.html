Part 1

What LaTeX does for a thesis

One idea per slide.

You write. LaTeX lays out the pages.

Margins, spacing, headings, page numbers, the contents: the class file sets them, following the OGE manual.

Each file has one job

  • metadata.texyour title page details
  • chapters/your words, one file per chapter
  • references.bibyour sources
  • rpi-thesis.clsthe formatting: leave it alone

Never type a number again

Give a figure a name with \label. Point at it with \ref.

LaTeX fills in "Figure 2.1", and renumbers it when things move.

An honest trade-off

LaTeX is good at

  • Long documents that stay stable
  • Numbering, contents, and lists
  • Citations and equations
  • Plain text files you keep

It asks you to

  • Learn about ten commands
  • Compile to see the result
  • Read an error now and then

Word is a fine choice too: OGE recommends its own Word template unless you are proficient in LaTeX. Pick the route that helps you finish.

Laptops open. Five minutes.

  3. Check the file listmain.tex sits at the top level.
  4. Click Recompile

You should see a title page reading YOUR THESIS TITLE, the contents, and three short chapters.

You can't break it.

Compiling reads your files and makes a PDF. It never changes your writing. An error means "fix this spot and compile again".

Put your name on it live

  1. Open the Settings builder on the website and fill in the form
  2. Click Copy settings
  3. In metadata.tex, replace the \thesissetup{...} block and all \committeemember lines
  4. Click Recompile and check the title page

Part 3

Write: a section, a figure, a table

Ordinary text plus a few commands.

How to read a .tex file

  • A command starts with \
  • Braces { } hold its text
  • % starts a note LaTeX ignores
  • A blank line starts a paragraph

A section live

In chapters/01-introduction.tex, paste this at the end:

\section{Why this matters}
This is my first paragraph, with a \textbf{bold} word
and an \emph{italic} one.

This is another paragraph.
Recompile: a new numbered section, and a new line in the contents. You never type the number.

A figure live

Upload a picture into figures/ as my-plot.png. Paste after a paragraph:

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/my-plot}
  \caption{Your full caption, ending with a period.}
  \label{fig:my-plot}
\end{figure}

In your text: Figure~\ref{fig:my-plot} shows ...

No picture handy? Write example-image in place of figures/my-plot. The caption goes below a figure.

A table live

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Your full caption, ending with a period.}
  \label{tab:my-table}
  \begin{tabular}{lr}
    \toprule
    Group & Value \\
    \midrule
    A & 12 \\
    B & 18 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}
  • The caption goes above a table
  • & moves to the next column; \\ ends the row
  • {lr}: one letter per column, left or right
  • Data in a spreadsheet? Paste the cells into Convert to LaTeX

Part 4

Cite a source

One list of sources. LaTeX does the numbering.

One key, two places

Each source has a key, like lamport1994.

It sits in references.bib and in \parencite{key} in your chapter.

Try the sample citation live

In chapters/01-introduction.tex, add a new paragraph:

This example cites the sample book~\parencite{lamport1994}.

Leave references.bib alone: the book is already in it.

Recompile: another [1] pointing to Lamport's book, already listed under REFERENCES.

Your own sources, with Zotero

  1. Install Zotero and its browser connector
  2. Save sources into one thesis collection
  3. Export the collection as BibLaTeX to references.bib
  4. On Overleaf, upload it over the old one
  5. Cite with \parencite{your-key} and compile
The key is the word after @article{ or @book{ in the entry.
Agree on a citation style with your advisor first. The sample's numbered style is not a department's style.

Zotero is free and optional. No Zotero? Paste a BibTeX entry by hand.

Part 5

When it breaks

Errors are normal. Most take a minute.

Read the first error

./chapters/01-introduction.tex:13: Undefined control sequence.
l.13 example~\parencit
                      {lamport1994}. Replace it with ...
1The file to open: chapters/01-introduction.tex, not main.tex 2The line: 13 3The message: LaTeX does not know a command, usually a typo 4Where it stopped: the end of the l.13 line, \parencit

Several errors? Fix only the first, then compile again. The later ones are often side effects.

Let the website explain it

  1. On Overleaf, click Logs and output files
  2. Copy the first error, or the whole log
  3. Paste it into Explain my error and click Explain the error
  4. Fix that one spot. Recompile.

Nothing is uploaded: the page reads the text in your browser.

Break it on purpose hands-on

5 min
  1. In chapters/01-introduction.tex, change \parencite to \parencit
  2. Recompile and open Logs and output files
  3. Find the file, the line, and the message
  4. Paste it into Explain my error
  5. Put the e back. Recompile. Fixed.
You should see "Undefined control sequence" in chapters/01-introduction.tex, on the line you changed.

Part 6

Before you submit

OGE's current requirements come first.

OGE's rules win

  • The manual, checklists, forms, and dates live on the OGE submission page. Start there each term.
  • Request OGE's preliminary formatting review before your defense. It is not available afterwards.
  • OGE reviews formatting. Its staff cannot debug LaTeX code: that is what the guide and its help form are for.
This toolkit is unofficial. It follows the manual as closely as it can, but nothing here replaces OGE's review.

Check your PDF

On the website, open Check your PDF and click Choose your PDF.

It measures paper size, page numbers, headings, side margins, the contents, and fonts against the manual.

Nothing is uploaded. It is not an OGE review: work through the guide's final PDF checklist too.

Each point gets one result

  • Looks right
  • Differs from the manual's wording
  • Look at this
  • Could not tell

Bring your draft

Keep your original untouched. Move one small section first.

  1. Paste it into Convert to LaTeX
  2. Choose Part of a chapter
  3. Click Copy, then paste below the \chapter and \label lines
  4. Recompile, and compare with your original
